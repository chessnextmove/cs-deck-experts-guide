Seasonal Deck Care: A Colorado Homeowner's Checklist

A deck in Colorado Springs works hard. It bakes under intense high-altitude sun in July, shrugs off afternoon hail in August, and carries heavy snow by January. Whether your deck was built for custom outdoor living, patched up with repairs over the years, or simply kept going with regular maintenance, a seasonal routine is what keeps it safe and attractive. Spring Inspection Checklist

Once the snow is gone and the ground has thawed, walk your deck slowly and look closely. Winter is when hidden damage starts.

  • Check the ledger board: make sure the board that attaches the deck to the house is tight, with no rusted or missing lag screws.
  • Inspect posts and footings: look for cracked concrete footings or posts that have shifted in soft, thawing soil.
  • Probe for rot: press a screwdriver into deck boards, joists, and stair stringers. Soft, spongy wood needs attention.
  • Tighten hardware: freeze-thaw cycles loosen nails and screws. Replace popped nails with exterior-grade screws.
  • Examine railings: grab each railing section and shake it. Any wobble should be reinforced before summer entertaining begins.
  • Wash the surface: sweep away grit and clean with a mild deck cleaner to remove mildew that grew under the snow.

Summer: Beating the High-Altitude Sun

At Colorado Springs elevations, UV intensity is roughly 25 percent stronger than at sea level, and it punishes unprotected wood. A water-repellent sealer with UV blockers is the best defense; plan to clean and reseal every one to two years, and sooner on south-facing decks. Test your finish by sprinkling water on the boards — if it soaks in instead of beading, it is time to reseal. Stick to morning or evening applications so the finish does not dry too fast in the midday heat. Move planters and rugs occasionally so the wood weathers evenly, and keep grills away from railings to prevent heat scorching.

Fall Prep Before the Snow Flies

Fall is your last chance to get the deck ready for months of snow and freeze-thaw. Clear leaves and needles from between boards so moisture can drain instead of sitting and rotting the wood. Clean the gutters above the deck so overflow does not pour onto the surface all winter. Trim back tree branches that could drop limbs in a heavy snow. If resealing is due, early fall is ideal: temperatures are mild and the wood is dry. Finally, drain and store hoses, and bring cushions and decor inside before the first hard freeze.

Winter Snow-Load Do's and Don'ts

Colorado snow is often light and fluffy, but wet spring storms can pile on surprising weight. Do clear heavy, wet snow with a plastic shovel or a push broom, always working with the grain of the boards to avoid scratching. Do clear a path to the stairs and keep drainage gaps open. Don't use a metal shovel or an ice pick, which gouges wood and composite alike. Don't spread rock salt or harsh deicers on wood — they dry and crack the fibers; use a calcium-based product sparingly or just sand for traction. And don't let snow drift several feet deep against the house, where meltwater can work into the ledger connection.

DIY vs. Time to Call a Professional

Many tasks are easy weekend projects: sweeping, washing, tightening screws, and applying a fresh coat of sealer. But some problems are safety issues. Call a professional when you find rotted or cracked structural members, a ledger board pulling away from the house, footings that have heaved or sunk, railings that will not stay rigid, or noticeable sagging and bouncing. Structural repairs require proper fasteners, load calculations, and code knowledge — getting them wrong risks collapse. When the job goes beyond routine upkeep, a local deck professional can assess the damage and recommend repair or replacement before it becomes dangerous.
